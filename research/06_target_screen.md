# Stage 1E: Target Screen and Selection

## Conclusion

**Cohort is the preferred target for the first detailed acquisition model.**

This is not a claim that Cohort is attractive at any price. It is the best combination of strategic fit, current financial quality, manageable transaction size and identifiable ownership benefits among the three candidates.

## Why Cohort ranks first

### Strategic fit: 5/5

Cohort adds a concentrated portfolio of sonar, naval communications, satellite terminals, electro-optical fire control, electronic warfare and specialist digital capability. BAE can use those products across maritime, land and electronic-system programmes. The link between product and BAE platform is clearer than for Avon and less dependent on capacity expansion than Chemring.

### Growth and financial quality: 4/5

FY2026 revenue increased 13%, adjusted operating profit increased 32%, margin reached 11.8% and the £618.8m order book extended to the mid-2030s. The score is not 5 because operating cash generation fell sharply as receivables increased and part of growth came from the EM Solutions acquisition.

### Valuation and affordability: 4/5

Cohort's approximate pre-premium EV / adjusted operating profit multiple is materially below Chemring's and below Avon's on the chosen full-year reference. A control premium will increase the multiple, but the absolute transaction value remains small relative to BAE.

### Synergy potential: 4/5

The clearest value comes from platform placement, international bidding, product integration and selective central-cost savings. The score is not 5 because customer neutrality and overlap with BAE may restrict revenue and cost synergies.

### Financing and leverage: 5/5

An illustrative 30% equity premium implies an equity purchase price of approximately £682m. This is modest relative to BAE's £4.2bn cash balance, more than £2bn annual free cash flow and £3.3bn FY2025 underlying EBIT. A mixed cash-and-debt structure should have a limited leverage effect.

### Execution and regulatory risk: 3/5

Cohort operates sensitive assets across several countries, EID has a Portuguese Government minority shareholder, and its decentralised culture is central to performance. Regulatory review appears manageable for a UK buyer, but integration mistakes could remove the agility BAE is acquiring.

## Why Chemring ranks second on strategic fit but third overall

Chemring's energetics and countermeasures businesses fit BAE's munitions, aircraft and naval portfolio well. Backlog and capacity investment provide strong growth visibility. However:

- its reference trading multiple is highest;
- the offer value would be substantially larger;
- BAE would inherit a significant capex and working-capital programme;
- Roke and other sensor activities overlap with BAE; and
- ownership could create supplier-neutrality concerns.

Chemring remains a credible alternative if diligence shows that securing energetics capacity has unusually high strategic value.

## Why Avon ranks second overall but is not selected

Avon has strong protective technology, improving margins and the smallest balance-sheet risk. It scores well on affordability and execution. However, its respiratory and helmet products are further from BAE's core platforms, electronics and weapons. Many commercial benefits may be available through partnership or distribution.

Avon would become more compelling if BAE explicitly wanted to build a soldier-protection and survivability business.

## Ownership model recommendation for Cohort

BAE should use a light-touch structure:

- preserve the seven operating businesses and their brands;
- retain key subsidiary management and technical teams;
- centralise only activities where scale clearly adds value;
- protect the ability to sell to other prime contractors;
- use BAE for international access, larger bids, funding and product integration; and
- link synergy targets to named programmes rather than broad cross-selling claims.
