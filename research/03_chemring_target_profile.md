# Stage 1B: Chemring Target Profile

## Purpose

This document assesses Chemring as a potential BAE Systems acquisition target before assigning a comparative score. The analysis separates Chemring's businesses because the strategic rationale is not identical across the group.

## Business overview

Chemring is a specialist defence, security and aerospace technology and manufacturing group with approximately 2,700 employees, production facilities in four countries and customers in more than 50 countries.

It reports through two segments:

1. **Countermeasures & Energetics**
2. **Sensors & Information**

Chemring states that more than 60% of its revenue is sole source. This indicates valuable qualified positions and barriers to entry, but it also means that the durability and transferability of individual programme positions must be reviewed carefully.

## Latest financial performance

| Measure | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | Change / observation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Revenue | £237.3m | £222.8m | Up 6.5% |
| Underlying operating profit | £24.5m | £26.5m | Down 7.5% |
| Underlying operating margin | 10.3% | 11.9% | Down 160 bps |
| Underlying operating cash flow | £15.9m | £32.3m | 42% cash conversion versus 83% in H1 2025 |
| Closing order book | £1,399m | £1,296m | Record level; up 8% as reported |
| Net debt | £144.5m | £93.3m | Higher due to growth capex and working capital |
| Net debt / rolling EBITDA | 1.47x | 0.98x | Still manageable, but debt is rising through the investment phase |

The order book covered approximately 91% of expected FY2026 revenue at 30 April 2026, with £1,139m scheduled for 2027 and beyond. This provides significant visibility, although delivery, working-capital and capacity-execution risks remain.

### Important interpretation

The headline is mixed rather than simply positive or negative:

- Revenue and backlog are growing.
- Countermeasures & Energetics is performing strongly.
- Group profit declined because Sensors & Information margins weakened.
- Cash conversion fell because inventory and safety stocks were built for later deliveries.
- Debt is increasing because Chemring is funding capacity expansion.

This is the profile of a business investing ahead of expected demand, not a mature company producing maximum near-term cash.

## Segment 1: Countermeasures & Energetics

### Products and market positions

The segment includes:

- expendable air and naval countermeasures;
- specialist high-grade military explosives;
- propellants and energetic materials;
- precision-engineered energetic and electro-mechanical devices; and
- space-launch initiators and release mechanisms.

Chemring describes itself as the global leader in advanced expendable countermeasures, with more than 65% market share, supplying 85% of NATO air fleets and 60% of NATO naval fleets.

### H1 2026 performance

| Measure | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | £142.1m | £130.2m |
| Underlying operating profit | £26.1m | £19.8m |
| Underlying operating margin | 18.4% | 15.2% |
| Order book | £1,274m | £1,214m |

Revenue increased 9.1% and underlying operating profit increased 31.8%. The order book covered approximately 81% of expected FY2027 revenue and 68% of expected FY2028 revenue.

Chemring is investing heavily in new Energetics capacity. Management expects the three expansion programmes, once complete, to add approximately £100m of annual revenue and £30m of annual operating profit from 2028.

### Potential fit with BAE

#### Energetics

BAE's Platforms & Services sector manufactures weapons and munitions, while Electronic Systems supplies precision-guided solutions. Chemring's explosives, propellants and energetic devices sit further upstream in that value chain.

Potential ownership benefits include:

- securing access to constrained munitions inputs;
- coordinating capacity investment with BAE's production plans;
- integrating energetic devices into BAE weapons and precision-guided products;
- improving procurement and production planning; and
- capturing margin currently earned by an external supplier.

This is primarily a **vertical-integration and capacity** rationale rather than entry into a completely new market.

#### Countermeasures

Chemring's products protect air and naval platforms from guided missiles. BAE manufactures and supports aircraft and naval platforms, creating potential for:

- closer integration of platform signatures, threat data and defensive countermeasures;
- earlier inclusion of Chemring products in platform design and upgrade programmes;
- access to BAE's international platform customers; and
- coordinated product development against evolving missile seekers.

However, Chemring already supplies a broad range of NATO platforms. BAE must show that ownership would create incremental value rather than merely internalise an existing supplier relationship.

## Segment 2: Sensors & Information

### Products and capabilities

The segment includes Roke and Chemring Sensors & Electronic Systems. Its capabilities include:

- communications, sensors and connectivity;
- cyber security and digital resilience;
- electronic warfare and signals intelligence;
- autonomy, AI and machine learning;
- data science and analytics;
- counter-UAS sensing and command systems;
- biological and explosive-threat detection; and
- counter-IED ground-penetrating radar.

Roke employs highly cleared scientists, engineers and consultants and serves UK defence, national-security and law-enforcement customers.

### H1 2026 performance

| Measure | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | £95.2m | £92.6m |
| Underlying operating profit | £9.6m | £16.1m |
| Underlying operating margin | 10.1% | 17.4% |
| Order book | £125m | £82m |

Revenue increased 2.8%, but profit and margin fell materially. Management attributed this to lower utilisation at Roke, business mix and early CORTEXA pre-production units. It retained highly cleared staff in anticipation of future opportunities, which protects capability but depresses short-term utilisation and profit.

### Potential fit with BAE

Possible benefits include:

- combining Roke's specialist engineers with BAE's larger customer and platform base;
- integrating counter-UAS sensing and command software into BAE platforms;
- applying Roke's AI, autonomy and sensor-fusion capabilities across air, maritime and land programmes; and
- adding biological detection, an area less prominent in BAE's existing portfolio.

The main concern is duplication. BAE already has substantial electronic-warfare, cyber, intelligence, AI, sensor and counter-UAS capabilities, including the Kirintec acquisition. BAE would need to identify specific Roke products, people or customer positions that are complementary rather than interchangeable with its existing operations.

## Potential synergies

### Cost synergies

Potential areas include:

- public-company and listing costs;
- duplicated corporate and administrative functions;
- combined procurement;
- selected facilities, IT and support activities; and
- reduced duplicated business-development expenditure.

Chemring reported £11.2m of unallocated corporate costs in H1 2026 and £19.3m in FY2025. These figures represent a starting reference, not an achievable synergy estimate. Some corporate functions would remain necessary inside BAE, and integration costs would occur before savings are realised.

### Revenue and operational synergies

Potential areas include:

- placing Chemring countermeasures on additional BAE-supported platforms;
- integrating Chemring energetics into BAE weapons and munitions;
- accelerating production-capacity deployment using BAE's customer visibility;
- cross-selling Roke products through BAE's international customer relationships; and
- using BAE platforms as routes to market for Roke autonomy and counter-UAS products.

Revenue synergies will be treated cautiously because defence sales depend on qualification, procurement decisions, export approvals and customer acceptance.

## Key risks and counterarguments

### 1. Supplier neutrality and customer conflicts

Chemring sells to a broad range of platform manufacturers and governments. Some customers may prefer an independent supplier rather than buying a critical input from BAE, a potential competitor or prime contractor.

### 2. Duplication

Roke and parts of Sensors & Information overlap with BAE Electronic Systems, Digital Intelligence and Kirintec. Paying a control premium for duplicate capabilities could destroy value.

### 3. Capacity-execution risk

The investment thesis partly depends on expansion projects delivering on time, on budget and at the expected utilisation. The forecast £100m revenue and £30m operating-profit contribution from 2028 is a management expectation, not a guaranteed outcome.

### 4. Safety and environmental exposure

Energetic materials create inherent process-safety, shutdown, regulatory and remediation risks. An incident could harm employees, interrupt production and create significant liabilities.

### 5. Working capital and near-term cash flow

Inventory, safety stocks and expansion capex are currently consuming cash. BAE would acquire both the future capacity and the remaining funding requirement.

### 6. Regulation and national security

A UK acquirer may be politically easier than a foreign buyer, but the transaction would still involve sensitive UK and US defence operations, security clearances, export controls and potentially competition review in narrowly defined countermeasure or energetic-product markets.

### 7. Retention of specialist staff

The value of Roke depends heavily on highly cleared scientists and engineers. Integration or cultural disruption could reduce the value BAE is trying to acquire.

## Preliminary strategic assessment

### Strongest part of the rationale

The strongest case is Chemring's Energetics and countermeasures portfolio:

- strong margins and backlog;
- qualified positions and barriers to entry;
- direct relevance to BAE weapons, aircraft and naval platforms;
- constrained industry capacity; and
- identifiable vertical-integration and platform opportunities.

### Weakest part of the rationale

The weakest part is the overlap between Roke/Sensors & Information and BAE's existing electronic, cyber and intelligence capabilities. The acquisition case must identify unique products, people or customer positions rather than assuming all technology overlap is valuable.

### Current conclusion

Chemring appears strategically credible enough to remain in the screen. It combines high-quality, capacity-constrained manufacturing assets with valuable technology. However, the case is not yet proven because:

- the purchase price and control premium have not been assessed;
- the value of the capacity programme must be tested;
- duplication within Sensors & Information may reduce the strategic benefit; and
- supplier-neutrality and regulatory risks may limit achievable synergies.

No numerical score is assigned until Cohort and Avon Technologies have been analysed under the same framework.
