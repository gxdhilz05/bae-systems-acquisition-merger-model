# Stage 2A: Cohort Forecast and Valuation

## Conclusion

Cohort remains the best strategic target, but the initial 30% premium is not supported by the base-case valuation.

The simple DCF produces a standalone value of approximately **1,076p per share**. Adding the present value of £12m of annual pre-tax cost synergies supports a maximum price of approximately **1,293p per share**, equal to a premium of about **13%** to the 24 September 2026 reference price. The illustrative 1,482p offer is therefore too high unless BAE identifies additional credible synergies or improves the operating case.

## Why the model is deliberately simple

The purpose is to understand and explain the acquisition logic, not to create a large model that hides the important judgments. The forecast uses five operating ideas:

1. Revenue grows from the FY2026 reported base.
2. Revenue growth gradually slows as the company becomes larger.
3. Operating margin improves toward management's long-term ambition.
4. Operating profit is converted into unlevered free cash flow using tax, depreciation, capital expenditure and working capital.
5. Forecast cash flows and a terminal value are discounted using WACC.

## Reported starting point

| Measure | FY2026 |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | £306.4m |
| Adjusted operating profit | £36.3m |
| Adjusted operating margin | 11.8% |
| Net funds | £2.2m |
| Capital expenditure | £17.6m |
| Cash generated from operations | £15.1m |

Management reported that 88% of expected FY2027 external revenue was on contract by mid-July 2026. It expects the operating margin to improve to the low teens in FY2027 and toward the mid-teens by the end of the decade. Management also targets double-digit earnings growth for FY2027 and the following two years.

## Base-case forecast assumptions

| Assumption | Base case | Reasoning |
|---|---:|---|
| FY2027 revenue growth | 8.0% | Strong order cover supports growth, but the model stays below the 13% FY2026 reported increase. |
| Annual reduction in growth | 1.0 percentage point | Growth fades as the revenue base becomes larger. |
| FY2027 operating margin | 12.5% | Consistent with management's low-teens statement. |
| Annual margin increase | 0.5 percentage point | Moves margin gradually toward 14.5% in FY2031, below the 15% ceiling used in the model. |
| Cash tax rate | 25.0% | UK corporation-tax reference and consistent with the acquisition model. |
| D&A as a percentage of revenue | 2.5% | Close to FY2026 depreciation including right-of-use assets. |
| Capital expenditure as a percentage of revenue | 4.0% | Below the elevated FY2026 level, which included investment in ELAC SONAR's new facility. |
| Working-capital investment | 10.0% of annual revenue increase | Normalises cash conversion instead of repeating the unusually large FY2026 receivables increase. |
| WACC | 9.0% | Illustrative required return for a smaller listed defence-technology company. |
| Terminal growth | 2.5% | Long-term nominal growth below the explicit forecast rate. |

## Core formulas

```text
Revenue = Prior-year revenue × (1 + revenue growth)

Adjusted operating profit = Revenue × operating margin

NOPAT = Adjusted operating profit × (1 - tax rate)

UFCF = NOPAT + D&A - capital expenditure - working-capital investment

Terminal value = FY2032 UFCF ÷ (WACC - terminal growth)

Enterprise value = PV of forecast UFCF + PV of terminal value

Equity value = Enterprise value - net debt
```

Cohort has net funds rather than net debt, so the £2.2m balance is added when moving from enterprise value to equity value.

## Synergy-supported price

The model values the £12m pre-tax run-rate synergies as a no-growth perpetuity:

```text
After-tax synergies = £12m × (1 - 25%) = £9m

Present value of synergies = £9m ÷ 9% = £100m
```

Adding £100m to standalone equity value produces the maximum supported price of approximately 1,293p per share. This is a valuation ceiling, not a target offer: paying the full synergy value to the seller leaves the buyer with none of the modeled benefit.

## Recommendation

BAE should continue diligence on Cohort because the strategic fit is strong, but it should not offer a 30% premium on the current assumptions. A price at or below approximately 1,300p would be more defensible, and BAE should seek a lower price if it wants to retain part of the synergy value for its own shareholders.
