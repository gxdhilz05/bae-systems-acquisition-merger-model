# Stage 2B: Simplified Purchase Accounting and Offer-Price Sensitivity

## Conclusion

Purchase accounting does not change the strategic case for Cohort, but it makes the earnings result less attractive. At the illustrative 30% premium:

- the equity purchase price is approximately £682.2m;
- the premium over Cohort's £186.4m book equity attributable to parent shareholders is approximately £495.7m;
- the model creates approximately £173.5m of new identifiable intangible assets and £365.6m of new goodwill;
- annual after-tax amortisation is approximately £13.0m;
- run-rate EPS accretion falls from approximately 0.6% before purchase accounting to approximately 0.1% after purchase accounting; and
- Year 1 EPS becomes approximately 0.5% dilutive after integration costs and amortisation.

The result supports the existing recommendation: BAE should pursue Cohort only at a disciplined price below approximately 1,300p per share.

## What purchase accounting means

When BAE buys Cohort, it does not simply place the whole purchase price on the balance sheet as one asset. The purchase price is allocated across the identifiable net assets acquired.

The simplified bridge uses four ideas:

1. **Book equity** is Cohort's accounting net asset value before the acquisition.
2. **New identifiable intangibles** represent assets such as customer relationships, technology and the order book that were not fully recognised at book value.
3. **Deferred tax liability** represents the assumed difference between the accounting value and tax value of the new intangibles.
4. **Goodwill** is the remaining premium after the other purchase-accounting adjustments.

## Base-case assumptions

| Assumption | Base case |
|---|---:|
| Cohort book equity attributable to parent shareholders | £186.4m |
| Premium allocated to new identifiable intangibles | 35.0% |
| New intangible useful life | 10 years |
| Tax rate | 25.0% |

The 35% allocation and ten-year life are illustrative. A real transaction would require specialist valuation work for technology, customer relationships, contracts and the order book.

## Core formulas

```text
Premium over book equity = Equity purchase price - Book equity

New identifiable intangibles = Premium over book equity × 35%

Deferred tax liability = New identifiable intangibles × 25%

New goodwill = Premium - New intangibles + Deferred tax liability

Annual amortisation = New intangibles ÷ 10 years
```

Goodwill is not amortised. It is tested for impairment. The new identifiable intangibles are amortised, reducing reported earnings even though the amortisation is non-cash.
