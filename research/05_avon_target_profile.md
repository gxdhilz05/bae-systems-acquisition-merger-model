# Stage 1D: Avon Technologies Target Profile

## Purpose

This document assesses Avon Technologies as a potential BAE Systems acquisition target. Avon is a specialist personal-protection business rather than a platform, electronics or weapons supplier, so the central issue is whether its differentiated protective technologies create enough ownership value for BAE.

## Business overview

Avon protects military, law-enforcement and first-responder users through two businesses:

| Business | Main products and positions |
|---|---|
| Avon Protection | Respirators, filters, powered-air systems, rebreathers and integrated chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear protection |
| Team Wendy | Ballistic and impact-protection helmets, liner and retention systems, and accessories |

The group states that its products protect more than four million users in over 70 markets. Its portfolio includes long-running US military respiratory programmes, NATO purchasing routes, NG IHPS and ACH Gen II helmet programmes, and a growing installed base that supports replacement filters, spares and accessories.

| Adjusted operating profit | $24.4m | $17.5m | Up 39.4% |
| Adjusted operating margin | 15.2% | 11.8% | Up 340 bps |
| Closing order book | $219.9m | $247.0m | Down 11.4% at constant currency |
| Orders received | $117.9m | $170.5m | Down 31.6% at constant currency |
| Cash conversion | 38% | 56% | Depressed by shipment and collection timing |
| Net debt excluding leases | $58.0m | $54.9m | Leverage remained below 1.0x |

The first-half profit improvement is significant. Management expects high-single-digit FY2026 revenue growth, an adjusted operating margin toward the top of the 14% to 16% range and cash conversion above 80%.

The weaker order intake and order book require context. Avon attributed the decline to timing of US helmet orders and temporary softness in US commercial helmet demand. These explanations are plausible, but future orders must be confirmed rather than treated as certain.

Avon Protection is the stronger business. Revenue increased 23% in H1 2026, helped by NATO mask demand, Ukraine-related orders, law-enforcement upgrades and a Canadian rebreather order. Team Wendy revenue declined 7.2% and remains a lower-margin operation despite manufacturing improvements.

## Potential fit with BAE

### User protection and survivability

Avon would add individual respiratory, CBRN and head-protection capabilities that are not prominent in BAE's current portfolio. Its products could complement BAE's land systems, military support and international government relationships.

Potential benefits include:

- offering protective equipment alongside selected land-system and support packages;
- using BAE's international government relationships to expand Avon products into more markets;
- combining platform survivability and individual protection in customer proposals; and
- applying BAE manufacturing and programme-management expertise to production scale-up.

### Installed-base and aftermarket economics

Avon's large installed base supports recurring demand for filters, spares, accessories and upgrades. That revenue has attractive characteristics and could benefit from BAE's global support infrastructure.

### Limits to strategic fit

Personal protective equipment is further from BAE's core platform, electronics, weapons and digital capabilities than Cohort or Chemring. BAE could distribute, partner with or bundle Avon products without owning the company. The acquisition case therefore needs stronger evidence that ownership would materially accelerate product development, market access or manufacturing performance.

## Potential synergies

Potential areas include:

- listed-company and duplicated corporate costs;
- international sales and export-support infrastructure;
- procurement and manufacturing-improvement methods;
- cross-selling through BAE government relationships; and
- selected bundling with land-system and support contracts.

Revenue synergies should be treated conservatively. Personal protection is often procured separately from major platforms, and customers may prefer competition among specialist suppliers.

## Key risks and counterarguments

1. **Strategic distance.** The products are valuable but less connected to BAE's main platforms and electronic systems.
2. **US programme concentration.** Team Wendy depends materially on US military helmet programmes, while Avon Protection has long-standing US respiratory positions.
3. **Order timing.** H1 2026 orders and backlog declined, making the timing of expected follow-on orders important.
4. **Team Wendy margin.** The 5.4% H1 margin remains well below Avon Protection and BAE's group margin.
5. **Manufacturing execution.** The recent improvement follows a multi-year transformation. Integration could interrupt progress.
6. **Product liability.** Life-protection products face certification, quality and liability risks.
7. **Customer neutrality.** Other primes and agencies may value Avon as an independent specialist.

## Preliminary strategic assessment

Avon is a high-quality specialist with improving margins, strong brands and a valuable installed base. It is also financially manageable for BAE. However, it offers fewer obvious platform and technology integration opportunities than Cohort or Chemring.

The most credible acquisition case would centre on building a broader survivability and soldier-protection portfolio. Without that explicit strategic ambition, partnership or distribution may capture much of the available value without paying a control premium.
