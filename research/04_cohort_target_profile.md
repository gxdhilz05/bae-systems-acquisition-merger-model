# Stage 1C: Cohort Target Profile

## Purpose

This document assesses Cohort as a potential BAE Systems acquisition target. Cohort is not one product company: it is a collection of seven specialist defence-technology businesses. The acquisition case therefore depends on whether BAE can preserve their agility while using its larger platform, customer and funding base to accelerate growth.

## Business overview

Cohort operates through two divisions and seven businesses:

| Division | Businesses | Main capabilities |
|---|---|---|
| Communications & Intelligence | MASS, MCL, EID, EM Solutions | Electronic warfare, digital services, training, surveillance technology, secure military communications and naval satellite communications |
| Sensors & Effectors | Chess Dynamics, ELAC SONAR, SEA | Electro-optical surveillance and fire control, submarine and surface-ship sonar, underwater communications, naval systems and anti-submarine-warfare sensing |

The group employs more than 1,700 people and operates in the UK, Germany, Portugal, Australia, Canada, Italy and the Netherlands. Its operating model deliberately gives subsidiaries substantial autonomy while providing financial strength, governance and shared market access.

## Potential fit with BAE

### Maritime and undersea systems

ELAC SONAR supplies submarine and surface-ship sonar and underwater communications. SEA supplies naval products including the KraitArray anti-submarine-warfare sensing system. EM Solutions adds high-end naval satellite-communications terminals.

These capabilities could strengthen BAE's naval platforms and support offer by:

- integrating sonar, underwater communications and satellite links earlier in ship and submarine design;
- offering more complete mission-system packages to international naval customers;
- using BAE's installed platform base to expand upgrades and through-life support; and
- combining platform, communications and sensing data into more integrated maritime solutions.

The counterargument is customer neutrality. Cohort sells equipment for platforms made by multiple primes. Some customers and shipbuilders may be less willing to buy from a BAE-owned supplier.

### Electronic warfare, surveillance and fire control

MASS provides electronic-warfare and digital services. MCL supplies electronic, tactical and surveillance technology. Chess Dynamics provides electro-optical surveillance, tracking and fire-control systems.

BAE could use these capabilities across land, maritime and air-defence programmes. The most credible value-creation route is not simply combining similar engineers. It is placing specialist products on a larger number of BAE-supported platforms and international programmes.

There is meaningful overlap with BAE Electronic Systems, Digital Intelligence and Kirintec. Product-level diligence is required to separate complementary intellectual property and customer positions from duplicate capability.

### Secure communications

EID designs military and naval communications systems, while EM Solutions produces naval satellite terminals. BAE could combine these products with its platform and electronic-systems portfolio and bring them into larger bids.

EID is only 80% owned by Cohort; the Portuguese Government retains 20%. The minority interest and the strategic nature of the assets increase governance and approval complexity.

## Potential synergies

### Cost synergies

Potential areas include:

- listed-company and duplicated central costs;
- selected procurement and supplier savings;
- shared export, compliance and bid-support resources; and
- removal of duplicated systems and public-company functions.

FY2026 central costs were approximately £6.7m. This is a reference point, not an achievable saving. BAE would still need governance, finance, security, export-control and operating support.

### Revenue and operational synergies

Potential areas include:

- placing Cohort naval sensors and communications on more BAE-supported vessels;
- incorporating Chess surveillance and fire-control products into BAE land and maritime offers;
- using BAE's global government relationships to expand Cohort's export reach;
- coordinating product roadmaps across sensors, communications and electronic warfare; and
- giving Cohort businesses capacity and balance-sheet support for larger contracts.

Revenue synergies are attractive but uncertain because defence qualification, customer choice, export approvals and incumbent supplier relationships can delay or prevent delivery.

## Key risks and counterarguments

1. **Loss of autonomy.** Cohort's model is built around small, responsive businesses. Heavy integration could slow decisions and cause talent loss.
2. **Capability overlap.** Electronic warfare, digital services, sensors and communications overlap with existing BAE activities.
3. **Customer neutrality.** Other prime contractors may be less willing to buy from a BAE-owned supplier.
4. **Working capital.** FY2026 cash generated from operations fell to £15.1m as receivables increased. Contract milestones and customer acceptance can create volatile cash conversion.
5. **Government and security approvals.** Assets in the UK, Germany, Portugal and Australia are strategically sensitive. EID's Portuguese Government minority stake adds complexity.
6. **Recent-acquisition integration.** Cohort paid £75m for EM Solutions in January 2025, funded partly by a £41m share placing. BAE would acquire a group still realising the benefits of its largest acquisition.
7. **Management retention.** Much of the value resides in specialist teams and subsidiary leadership.

## Preliminary strategic assessment

Cohort is a strong candidate because it offers a concentrated collection of naval, sensing, communications and electronic-warfare capabilities at a transaction size that would be readily financeable for BAE. The clearest ownership advantage is BAE's ability to place Cohort products on more platforms and support larger international bids.

The acquisition should not be justified as a conventional cost-cutting integration. The investment case is strongest if BAE preserves the subsidiary model, protects customer neutrality where possible and focuses integration on market access, funding, security, compliance and selected product roadmaps.
