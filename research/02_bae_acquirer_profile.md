# Stage 1A: BAE Systems Acquirer Profile

## Purpose

Before screening Chemring, Cohort and Avon Technologies, the analysis must establish what BAE Systems already owns, what it is trying to achieve and what constraints apply to an acquisition.

The central question is not simply whether a target is a strong business. It is:

> What could BAE achieve under ownership of the target that it could not achieve as efficiently through internal development, a supply agreement or a partnership?

## Latest financial position

The latest available results are for the six months ended 30 June 2026.

| Measure | H1 2026 | Interpretation for the acquisition screen |
|---|---:|---|
| Sales | £15,772m | BAE is far larger than any of the proposed targets. The acquisition would be a focused portfolio decision rather than a transformational combination. |
| Underlying EBIT | £1,701m | Strong operating earnings provide capacity, but acquisition returns must compete with internal investment and shareholder returns. |
| Return on sales | 10.8% | A target with structurally lower margins needs a credible improvement case or unusually strong strategic value. |
| Free cash flow | £1,791m | Cash generation supports flexibility, although H1 benefited from material customer advances and should not be treated as a normal recurring half-year run-rate. |
| Order intake | £16.4bn | Demand remains strong across the portfolio. BAE does not need an acquisition to compensate for weak organic demand. |
| Order backlog | £84.0bn | Long-term visibility supports investment capacity, while also creating substantial execution and production commitments. |
| Cash and cash equivalents | £4,200m | Cash is a potential funding source, but not all cash can be spent because BAE needs liquidity for operations, programmes and shareholder commitments. |
| Net debt excluding leases | £3,173m | BAE has meaningful existing debt following recent investment and M&A. Any new transaction must preserve balance-sheet flexibility and its investment-grade rating. |

BAE's 2026 guidance, following its half-year results, was for sales growth of 8% to 10%, underlying EBIT growth of 10% to 12%, underlying EPS growth of 11% to 13% and free cash flow above £2.0bn. The company also states that capital allocation must balance investment in the business, dividends, M&A and buybacks while maintaining an investment-grade rating.

### Why this matters

BAE already owns extensive capabilities in electronic warfare, sensors, precision-guided weapons, combat platforms, cyber and space. Therefore, product overlap is not automatically evidence of strategic fit.

For each target we need to distinguish between:

- **Useful adjacency:** technology that strengthens BAE's existing products or fills a genuine gap;
- **Scalable ownership benefit:** products BAE can place on more platforms, sell to more customers or manufacture more efficiently;
- **Redundant overlap:** capabilities that largely duplicate what BAE already possesses; and
- **Partnership alternative:** technology BAE could access without paying a takeover premium.

## BAE's current strategic priorities

BAE identifies three broad priorities:

1. Drive operational excellence.
2. Improve competitiveness and efficiency continuously.
3. Advance and further leverage technology.

Current investment is focused on accelerating innovation, improving efficiency and expanding capacity. BAE reported increasing self-funded research and development in high-growth areas and elevated capital investment in facilities, combat vehicles and precision-guided munitions.

This suggests that a credible acquisition should do at least one of the following:

- add differentiated technology faster than internal development;
- expand capacity in an area with visible customer demand;
- improve access to customers, programmes or geographies;
- strengthen an existing BAE platform through integration of the target's products; or
- add a focused business that can grow faster under BAE's ownership.

## Evidence from previous acquisitions

### Space & Mission Systems

BAE completed its acquisition of Ball Aerospace in February 2024 for approximately $5.5bn in cash, creating the Space & Mission Systems business. The acquisition added space, science and defence technology and more than 5,200 US employees. BAE integrated the business into Electronic Systems and is now reporting strong growth in military and national-security space programmes.

**Screening lesson:** BAE is willing to make a large acquisition when it adds a strategically important capability with strong customer alignment. However, the transaction also increased debt, so it does not prove that BAE should pay any price for another target.

### Kirintec

BAE acquired Kirintec in September 2024. Kirintec added UK cyber and electromagnetic activities, counter-IED, counter-uncrewed-air-system and electronic-warfare products.

**Screening lesson:** BAE also uses smaller bolt-on acquisitions to add complementary technologies. This is especially relevant when assessing whether a proposed target fills a new gap or overlaps with capabilities BAE has already acquired.

## Preliminary acquisition criteria

The evidence above implies the following criteria. These are analytical inferences rather than stated promises by BAE.

1. **Differentiated capability:** The target should add defensible technology, expertise or customer access.
2. **Platform leverage:** BAE should be able to use the capability across its aircraft, maritime, land, electronic or digital portfolio.
3. **Visible demand:** The target should be exposed to funded priorities rather than speculative demand alone.
4. **Ownership advantage:** Full ownership should create more value than a partnership or supplier relationship.
5. **Return potential:** Growth, margins and synergies must justify the takeover premium.
6. **Manageable integration:** The transaction should not distract from BAE's large existing programme commitments.
7. **Balance-sheet discipline:** Funding must preserve liquidity, financial flexibility and an investment-grade rating.

## What this means for the target screen

The strategic-fit analysis for each target will answer four questions:

1. Which BAE sector would own the target?
2. What capability would the target add rather than merely duplicate?
3. How could BAE create additional value from that capability?
4. Why is acquisition preferable to internal development or partnership?

No target will receive a high strategic-fit or synergy score unless these four questions have clear answers.

## Limitations at this stage

- H1 2026 free cash flow includes material customer advances and should not be annualised mechanically.
- Cash on the balance sheet is not identical to cash available for an acquisition.
- The analysis has not yet estimated transaction prices or pro forma leverage.
- Potential capability gaps are preliminary until the three targets have been researched consistently.
- A target's current market valuation and likely control premium may change the strategic conclusion.
